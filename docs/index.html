01 — Anatomy

Inside a single glyph

One character, four readings of the same font data. The coverage mask is the rasteriser's 26.6 fixed-point output. The outline is the raw Bézier path. The extrusion gives that path depth and light. The metrics overlay shows the em square the engine measured. A character the demo face does not carry — Chinese, say — is shown in the page's own face, so the four panels are still four readings of one glyph.

03 — Type with the engine

A keyboard, rendered live

Every keystroke is a decoded glyph drawn by the rasteriser, in the demo font — and set in the page's face when the demo font has no such character. The engine is the typesetter here, not a webfont.

04 — Extrude

Pull a glyph into space

The coverage mask becomes a solid. Drag to orbit; the light is a fixed direction, so the form turns through it. Depth and lighting are live.

05 — Pour

A tray of castings

Every piece below is a glyph this page decoded, sampled into convex hulls and handed to a rigid-body solver. Drag one: it hangs from a spring, swings, and keeps the speed of your hand when you let go. It lands on its neighbours, and on the button and the caption, which are solids too. Depth, light and shadow are drawn from the same outline, so the same body can be read five ways.

cast 07 / 20

Solid: the outline extruded towards the light, one shadow per piece. This line and the button are solids. The letters rest on them.
