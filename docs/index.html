Parsing, metrics and glyph rasterisation, compiled from MoonBit to JavaScript. This page runs the engine in your browser — decode a font, watch a glyph resolve into pixels, then pull it into three dimensions. No server. No upload. No network.

01 — Anatomy

Inside a single glyph

One character, four readings of the same font data. The coverage mask is the rasteriser's 26.6 fixed-point output. The outline is the raw Bézier path. The extrusion gives that path depth and light. The metrics overlay shows the em square the engine measured.

02 — Bring your own font

Drop a font, watch it think

The file never leaves your machine. It is decoded by moonfont in the page, then rasterised on demand. Try any .ttf or .otf — a font you love, a font you made, a font you suspect.

03 — Type with the engine

A keyboard, rendered live

Every keystroke is a decoded glyph drawn by the rasteriser, in the demo font. The engine is the typesetter here, not a webfont.

04 — Extrude

Pull a glyph into space

The coverage mask becomes a solid. Drag to orbit; the light is a fixed direction, so the form turns through it. Depth and lighting are live.

05 — Sort

A tray of castings

Every shape below is a glyph this page decoded. Drag one, throw it, and it falls, turns and stacks against its neighbours and against the page itself: the button and the caption in the tray are solid, so letters settle on them. The collision shape is the coverage mask the rasteriser produced, sampled into a handful of circles; the outline drawn over it is the path the engine handed out. Nothing here is a webfont.
